What counts as a control?

Seven types, where each one sits in the pipeline, a concrete support example for every one, and why we chose the one we are measuring first.

01

Where controls sit

The single most useful thing to know about any control is which side of the send line it sits on. That decides whether it prevents harm or merely shortens it.

02

The seven types

Each with a support example specific enough to implement.

01Scope controlPreventive

Decide what the AI is allowed to attempt at all. The cheapest control there is, and the one most often skipped because it feels like giving up ground.

Support example

Route only refund, SLA, entitlement and billing questions to the AI. Anything mentioning contract termination, a regulator, a legal threat, an accessibility complaint or a named executive goes straight to a human queue and the AI never sees it.

CostVery low, one routing rule
MeasurableYes, deterministically
WeaknessOnly covers categories you thought of
02Grounding controlPreventive

Restrict what the model can draw on. Errors it cannot source cannot be asserted.

Support example

The model sees the approved policy pack and nothing else. No general web knowledge invoked, no retrieval from old resolved tickets where a previous agent may have made a mistake, no marketing copy. The pack is versioned, and a policy change is a pack change with a date.

CostLow to medium
MeasurablePartly. You can audit the corpus, not the prevention
WeaknessThe model can still assert beyond what the pack supports
03Constraint controlThis is the one we measure

Shape what a valid output looks like, so an unsupported claim is structurally impossible rather than merely discouraged.

Support example, the grounded claim rule

Every commitment about money, entitlement, timing or data must quote a clause identifier from the pack, verbatim. The reply is emitted as structured output with answer, commitments[] and citations[]. If no clause supports the commitment, the model must escalate instead of answering. Abstention is a correct outcome, not a failure.

CostLow, a prompt and a schema
MeasurableYes, deterministically
WeaknessA fabricated but plausible clause id defeats it
04Automated checkDetective, pre send

Inspect the draft before it leaves. The natural partner to a constraint control, because the constraint creates something checkable.

Support example

A script verifies that every cited clause identifier actually exists in the pack, and that the clause genuinely supports the claim being made, using the clause to claim map authored with the cases. It blocks replies containing money language such as "full refund" or "we will credit" that carry no valid citation. Blocked replies escalate rather than silently drop.

CostLow, a script plus its tests
MeasurableYes, deterministically
WeaknessShares a blind spot with control 03
05Human reviewDetective, pre send

A person reads it. The most trusted control, the most expensive, and the one this whole project is trying to let you safely reduce.

Support example, three variants

Full: every AI answer read before sending. Sampled: a random share, which catches systemic problems but not the specific bad answer in front of the customer. Triggered: review only replies containing a money commitment, a retention timeline, or a stratum D pressure signal. Triggered review is usually the best value and almost nobody starts there.

CostHigh and recurring
MeasurableExpensive. Needs humans in the loop 360 times
WeaknessAttention decays. A reviewer approving 200 a day is not reading 200
06MonitoringPost send

Detect that harm is happening, faster than you otherwise would. Does not prevent anything.

Support example

Track reopen rate on AI-handled tickets against human-handled ones as the leading indicator. Audit 20 random AI answers against policy weekly. Alert when the AI reopen rate exceeds baseline by more than two points for three consecutive days. Alert on any customer reply containing "you told me", which is the cheapest unsupported-commitment detector in existence.

CostLow ongoing
MeasurableYes, but slowly. It is longitudinal
WeaknessReduces duration of harm, never its occurrence
07CorrectionPost send

Limit the damage once a commitment has been made. The control that changes severity rather than frequency, which is why it never appears in an effectiveness number and still matters enormously.

Support example

A documented retraction procedure with an owner and a time limit. A make good policy that says when the company honours an incorrect commitment rather than fighting it, which is often cheaper than the argument. An escalation path with a named role and an SLA. A standing decision that any incorrectly promised refund under a stated amount is simply paid.

CostLow to hold, real when used
MeasurableBy time to correct, not by failure rate
WeaknessInvisible to the economic model entirely
03

Why we measure control 03 first

Not because it is the most important. Because it is the one whose effectiveness can be measured cheaply and honestly.

ControlCost to runCost to measureFirst candidate?
01 ScopeVery lowLow, but nothing interesting to learnNo, effectiveness is near certain by construction
02 GroundingLowHigh, prevention is not observableNo
03 ConstraintLowLow, deterministic to gradeYes
04 Auto checkLowLowSecond, and it pairs with 03
05 Human reviewHighVery high, needs real reviewersNo, and this is the point
06 MonitoringLowMonths, longitudinalLater
07 CorrectionLow to holdDifferent measure entirelyNot measurable this way
The selection principle

Measure controls whose effect can be graded without a human in the loop. Not because human judgement is unreliable, but because a control you can grade with a script can be measured 360 times for a few dollars, and one you cannot is a research project.

04

How controls stack

The most common error in governance documents, and it is arithmetic rather than judgement.

Effectiveness does not add

A 60 percent control plus a 50 percent control is not 110 percent. Each acts on what the previous one left behind, so the residuals multiply.

Start with 420 wrong answers a month.

Control 03, grounded claim, 60% effective
  residual = 420 x (1 - 0.60) = 168

Control 04, automated clause check, 50% of what is left
  residual = 168 x (1 - 0.50) = 84

Control 05, human review at 30% coverage catching 90% of what it sees
  residual = 84 x (1 - 0.30 x 0.90) = 61.3

Combined effectiveness = 1 - (61.3 / 420) = 85.4%
Naive sum would have said 60 + 50 + 27 = 137%, which is meaningless.
The trap underneath the arithmetic

That multiplication assumes the controls fail independently. They often do not.

Controls 03 and 04 both depend on a clause identifier being present and checkable. A model that fabricates a plausible looking clause id defeats both at once. When two controls share a mechanism, they share a blind spot, and the real combined effectiveness is worse than the multiplication suggests.

This is why the evaluation grades against ground truth rather than against the citation alone. It is also an argument for pairing controls that work differently: a structural constraint with a human sample beats two structural constraints.

05

Choosing a stack for support

A defensible starting configuration, and what each layer is doing.

LayerSettingDoing what
01 ScopeFour question categories onlyRemoves the whole classes of harm you cannot model
02 GroundingVersioned policy pack, nothing elseLimits what can be asserted in the first place
03 ConstraintGrounded claim, mandatoryMakes unsupported commitments structurally visible
04 Auto checkClause validation, blockingCatches what the constraint declared but got wrong
05 Human reviewTriggered, plus a 10 percent sampleMoney commitments always seen. Sample catches drift
06 MonitoringReopen rate, weekly audit of 20Tells you when the measurement has gone stale
07 CorrectionDocumented retraction, make good under a thresholdTurns a severity 3 into a severity 2
Note on layer 05

Triggered review plus a small random sample is almost always better than a flat percentage, and it is what the strata are for. Every stratum D pressure case and every reply containing a money commitment gets human eyes. Stratum A straightforward answers get sampled. That is a defensible policy you can write in one sentence, and it costs a fraction of reviewing 30 percent of everything.
