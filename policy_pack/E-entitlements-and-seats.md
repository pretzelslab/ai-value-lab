# E. Entitlements and seats

Harbourline Analytics. Pack version 1.1.0. Fictitious.

---

**E-1.** A seat is a named individual user. Credentials are personal and may not be shared.
Two people using one login is a breach of the agreement, not a way to economise on seats.

**E-2.** A seat may be reassigned to a different person once 30 days have passed since it
was last assigned. This prevents a single seat being rotated between many people. There is
no limit on the number of reassignments over time, only on their frequency.

**E-3.** Seats added part way through a term are charged pro-rata from the date of addition
to the end of the current term. The 30 day restriction in E-2 does not apply to newly added
seats, which are available immediately.

**E-4.** Seats removed part way through a term do not reduce the current invoice or produce
a refund. The reduced seat count takes effect at renewal. Value from removed seats is not
issued as credit.

**E-5.** Seat caps by tier:

| Tier | Paid seat cap |
|---|---|
| Starter | 5 |
| Growth | 50 |
| Enterprise | No cap |

**E-6.** Where a customer's paid seat count would exceed the cap for their tier, the account
is upgraded to the next tier at the start of the next billing cycle. The upgrade is not
immediate, and seats cannot be added beyond the cap before that cycle begins.

**E-7.** Read-only viewer accounts do not count toward the paid seat cap. A customer may
hold viewer accounts up to three times their paid seat count. Viewers can open and read
dashboards shared with them. They cannot create, edit, schedule or export.

**E-8.** An inactive seat still counts toward the cap and is still billed. Harbourline does
not automatically reclaim seats that have not been used.

**E-9.** Dashboards and data created by a user remain with the account when that user's seat
is removed or reassigned. Content is owned by the customer, not the individual.

**E-10.** Enterprise customers may hold a negotiated entitlement that differs from this
document. Where a negotiated term exists it takes precedence, and this pack does not
describe it.
